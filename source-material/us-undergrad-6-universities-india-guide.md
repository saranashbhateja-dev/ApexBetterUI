# Applying to MIT, Stanford, Harvard, Caltech, Yale & Princeton — Undergraduate Guide for an Indian Student

**Research date:** 24 October 2026
**Target entry:** Fall 2028 → you apply in the **2027-28 cycle** (Aug 2027 – Jan 2028 deadlines, decisions Dec 2027 – Apr 2028)
**Written for:** an Indian applicant (CBSE/ICSE/State-board or similar) wanting maximum financial aid ("100% scholarship")

---

## 0. HOW TO READ THIS FILE (important)

**Confidence tags**

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ | Read directly on the university's own website (fetched 24 Oct 2026) |
| ⚠️ | From a third-party site (blog, agency, aggregator) or an older official page — **verify before relying on it** |
| ❓ | I could not confirm this — you must check the official page |

**Three things you must understand before reading anything else**

1. **Dates are from the 2026-27 cycle** (for Fall 2027 entry). Your cycle is one year later. Universities almost never move deadlines by more than a few days, but **re-check every date in August 2027**.
2. **Third-party sites often contradict the official sites.** Example: one popular guide says Stanford *requires* TOEFL/IELTS — Stanford's own page says it does **not**. Another says Caltech is "test-free" — Caltech's own page says SAT/ACT **are required**. Where they conflict, this file uses the official site and flags the conflict.
3. **"100% scholarship" does not exist as a merit prize at any of these six.** None of them give merit scholarships. All six give **need-based aid** only. "100% scholarship" in practice means *the university covers 100% of the gap between its cost and what your family can pay*. If your family's assessed contribution is zero, aid can cover essentially the whole cost; if the family can pay part, you pay that part. See Section 8.

## 2. MIT — Massachusetts Institute of Technology

### 2.1 Who you are in MIT's eyes
- MIT calls you an **international applicant** (no US citizenship/permanent residency), regardless of where you live ✅
- International and domestic applicants **apply the same way and at the same time** ✅
- Most applicants are 17–19 ✅
- If you've already enrolled at another university, you must apply as a *transfer* ✅

### 2.2 Application platform & fee
- **MIT's own application portal** (apply.mitadmissions.org) — **not** the Common App ✅
- Application fee: **$75**, or a fee waiver ✅
- You may apply **only once per entry year** — Early Action *or* Regular Action ✅

### 2.3 Deadlines (2026-27 cycle shown)

| Item | Early Action | Regular Action |
|---|---|---|
| Application | **Nov 1** | **Jan 4** |
| Decision | mid-December | mid-March |
| SAT/ACT must be taken before | Nov 30 | Dec 31 |
| English test (RA) | — | by Jan 31 (RA applicants may use January test dates) |
| **Financial aid forms** | **Nov 30** | **Feb 15** |
| Midyear update (FUN form) | mid-February | mid-February |
| Reply to MIT | May 1 | May 1 |

(Source ✅: mitadmissions.org/apply/firstyear/deadlines-requirements)

### 2.4 Everything MIT requires ✅
1. **MIT first-year application form** (biographical info, essays, activities, academics, recommenders, test scores)
2. **Secondary School Report (SSR) with transcript**, sent by your school counselor (or an equivalent school official if you have no counselor)
   - Transcript should include **three years of grades** and results of external exams like **CBSE** ✅
   - MIT does **not** convert your grades to a US GPA ✅
   - Predicted grades are accepted; final grades only after admission ✅
3. **Two teacher letters of recommendation** — one from math/science, one from humanities/social science/language (recommended split) ✅
   - Optional: counselor letter, school profile, one supplemental letter
   - You don't have to wait for recommenders before submitting ✅
4. **SAT or ACT** (see 2.5)
5. **English proficiency test** if applicable (see 2.6)
6. **$75 fee / waiver**
7. **Optional creative portfolios** via SlideRoom — for researchers, makers, performers, visual artists; "truly optional… only if significant to you and relevant" ✅
8. **Interview** with an MIT Educational Counselor, *if one is available*; if not, it's waived and doesn't hurt you ✅
9. **February Updates & Notes (FUN) form** with midyear grades (deferred EA / admitted students) ✅
10. **Enrollment reply by May 1** ✅

**No required high-school classes** ✅ (but MIT expects minimum competence in fields it will continue teaching).

### 2.5 Exams — SAT/ACT ✅
- **SAT or ACT required** for first-year applicants. ACT Writing/Science and the SAT optional essay are **not** required. Paper and digital SAT both accepted.
- **No cutoff scores** published ✅ (context-based reading)
- **Superscoring:** MIT primarily considers your highest score in each section across sittings ✅
- **Self-reporting:** you don't need to officially send scores at application; MIT verifies at enrollment ✅ (don't enter a self-calculated superscore as one sitting; report real sittings)
- JEE is **not** accepted as a substitute ⚠️ (SAT/ACT stated as compulsory by official page; JEE-specific statement from a secondary source)
- Typical admit SAT Math middle-50% ≈ 780–800 ⚠️ (third-party)

### 2.6 English proficiency ✅
- Strongly recommended if you've used English for **fewer than 5 years** or **don't speak English at home or in school**
- Accepted: TOEFL, IELTS, Duolingo English Test (DET), PTE Academic, Cambridge C1 Advanced/C2 Proficiency

| Test | Minimum | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| TOEFL iBT (taken before 21 Jan 2026) | 90 | 100 |
| TOEFL (taken on/after 21 Jan 2026, new 1–6 scale) | 5 | 5.5 |
| IELTS | 7 | 7.5 |
| PTE Academic | 65 | 70 |
| Cambridge C1/C2 | 185 | 190 |
| Duolingo (DET) | 120 | 125 |

### 2.7 Essays ⚠️
- MIT has **no single personal statement**; it asks a set of **short-answer prompts that change each year** (third-party sources say about four, ~100–200 words each) ⚠️
- ❓ The 2027-28 prompts won't exist until MIT publishes them (~August 2027). Check mitadmissions.org/apply/firstyear/essays-activities-academics.

### 2.8 Financial aid & "100% scholarship" ✅
- MIT is **committed to meeting 100% of demonstrated financial need for international students**, same process as domestic ✅
- Admission is need-blind for internationals ⚠️ (widely stated; verify wording on MIT's current page)
- **No merit scholarships** — the **MIT Scholarship** is need-based ✅
- **Two documents** ✅:
  1. **CSS Profile**, MIT code **3514**, plus MIT-specific supplemental questions
  2. **Parents' tax returns / income documents** uploaded to **College Board IDOC**
- For the CSS Profile you'll need ✅: parents' income tax returns or wage statements **from two years ago** (prior-prior year), other earnings records, current bank balances, investment records, untaxed income records
- If parents live outside the US, submit **their country's** tax return with an English translation if needed — **translation need not be professional** ✅
- If parents are separated/divorced, **each parent completes their own CSS Profile** and provides income documents ✅
- IDOC → MIT can take **up to two weeks** ✅; documents must go through IDOC (not directly to MIT) ✅
- Track documents in the MIT application portal ("Track Financial Aid Documents") — allow ~10 business days ✅
- **Aid deadlines:** Nov 30 (EA), Feb 15 (RA) ✅
- Cost of attendance ≈ $92,760 for 2026-27 ⚠️ (third-party)
- ❓ Whether MIT's aid packages include a work/loan "self-help" expectation: check sfs.mit.edu/undergraduate-students/types-of-aid

### 2.9 Selectivity (context)
- Class of 2028: 28,232 applications, 1,284 offers (**4.5%**); **11% international citizens from 59 countries** ✅ (facts.mit.edu)
- Class of 2029: 29,281 applicants → 1,334 admits (4.6%); international: 6,926 applicants → 136 admits (**≈2.0%**) ⚠️

### 2.10 MIT-specific document checklist
- [ ] SAT or ACT score(s) (before Nov 30 / Dec 31)
- [ ] English test score (if you're in the "recommended" group)
- [ ] School transcripts (Class IX–XII, incl. board results / predicted), via counselor
- [ ] SSR from counselor
- [ ] 2 teacher letters (1 math/science + 1 humanities)
- [ ] Short-answer essays
- [ ] Activities list
- [ ] $75 fee or waiver
- [ ] Optional: SlideRoom portfolio (research/maker)
- [ ] CSS Profile (3514) + IDOC parental tax docs
- [ ] Interview (if offered)
- [ ] FUN form (Feb) with midyear grades

## 3. STANFORD UNIVERSITY

### 3.1 Platform & fee
- **Common App**, **Coalition on SCOIR**, or **QuestBridge** ✅
- Application fee: **$100** or fee-waiver request ✅
- **Lifetime cap: only three applications total** to Stanford (first-year + transfer combined) ✅
- Stanford does **not** work with paid agents/credentialing services — you must prepare materials yourself ✅

### 3.2 Deadlines (2026-27 cycle)

| Round | Common App deadline | Decision | Reply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restrictive Early Action (standard) | **Nov 1** | by mid-December | May 1 |
| Restrictive Early Action (with optional arts portfolio) | **Oct 15** (portfolio Oct 20) | by mid-December | May 1 |
| Regular Decision (standard) | **Jan 5** | early April | May 1 |
| Regular Decision (with arts portfolio) | **Dec 5** (portfolio Dec 10) | early April | May 1 |

Midyear transcript due **Feb 15** ✅ (source: admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year)

### 3.3 Required components ✅
1. **Common Application** (personal essay + Stanford questions ❓ — prompts change yearly)
2. **$100 fee or waiver**
3. **SAT or ACT** (required for international applicants too ✅)
4. **School Report form + counselor letter of recommendation** (if no counselor, a principal/teacher can submit)
5. **Official transcript(s)/academic results**, plus **Midyear transcript** by Feb 15
6. **Letters of recommendation from two academic teachers**

### 3.4 International-specific rules ✅
- Requirements are **the same** for everyone, but **financial aid policy differs**
- **Translation:** transcripts and teacher letters must be in English. Documents not in English need translation — **you cannot translate them yourself** (ask your English teacher/school official) and the **original must also be submitted** ✅
- Official documents with marks/predictions must be sent **directly from your school** ✅
- No required courses or minimum scores; **no GPA conversion needed** ✅
- You must hold a secondary-school diploma (or equivalent) **by the time you enroll** (not at application) ✅
- Stanford starts only in **Fall quarter** ✅
- If you've already earned credit at a degree-granting program after high school, you must apply as a transfer ✅

### 3.5 Exams
- **SAT/ACT: required** ✅
- **English proficiency exam: NOT required** — you may self-report TOEFL/IELTS/etc. if you want, but "fluency in English is a prerequisite" ✅
  - ⚠️ Some third-party guides say Stanford requires TOEFL/IELTS; Stanford's own international page says it does **not**.

### 3.6 Financial aid ⚠️ This is the strictest of the six for internationals
- **Need-aware for international citizens** ✅: your request for aid **is a factor in admission**, "making the process more selective"
- Stanford **will meet the full need of all admitted students who requested aid**, regardless of citizenship ✅
- **No merit scholarships** (only a limited number of athletic) ✅
- **You must request aid on your admission application.** If you don't, you are **not eligible to apply for aid at any time during your four years** ✅ (exception: if your citizenship status changes)
- To receive university scholarship funds you must obtain an **SSN or ITIN** (not needed for the application) ✅
- **Forms:** CSS Profile, Stanford code **4704** ✅ (confirmed on Stanford's QuestBridge partner page), plus the **International Student Supplement to the CSS Profile** (official Stanford PDF exists at financialaid.stanford.edu) ✅
- ⚠️ A third-party site mentions a free "ISAFA" alternative to the CSS fee; I did **not** find this on Stanford's official pages. Contact Stanford's financial aid office before assuming it.
- ⚠️ I did **not** confirm 2027-28 aid deadlines (historically they follow the admission round: Nov-ish for REA, Feb 15 for RD) — check financialaid.stanford.edu.
- Stanford's own average-aid data ✅: average scholarship/grant for need-based aid recipients in the current freshman class **$74,310** (incl. **$66,259** avg Stanford scholarship). Net cost for families with income under $75,000 averaged ~**$4,414/year** — **but international students are excluded from this table**, so don't treat it as your number.

### 3.7 Stanford-specific document checklist
- [ ] SAT/ACT
- [ ] Common App + essays + Stanford questions
- [ ] $100 fee or waiver
- [ ] School Report + counselor letter
- [ ] Official transcripts (translated if needed; original also)
- [ ] Midyear transcript (Feb 15)
- [ ] 2 academic teacher letters (translated by school if needed)
- [ ] Tick "yes" to financial aid on the application (**irreversible decision**)
- [ ] CSS Profile (4704) + International Student Supplement + parent income docs
- [ ] Optional: arts portfolio (earlier deadlines), interview (❓ check page)

## 4. HARVARD UNIVERSITY

### 4.1 Platform & fee
- **Common Application** (also a QuestBridge partner) ✅
- Fee: ❓ paid via Common App (third-party sources say $85). **If the fee is a hardship and you plan to apply for aid, it's waived** ✅; requesting a waiver does not disadvantage you ✅
- **No quotas** for international applicants — everyone is in one pool ✅

### 4.2 Deadlines (2026-27 cycle)

| Round | Deadline | Aid forms |
|---|---|---|
| Restrictive Early Action | **Nov 1** | CSS Profile + IDOC by **Nov 1** |
| Regular Decision | **Jan 1** | CSS Profile + IDOC by **Feb 1** |

(Counselors get one extra week for school materials ✅.) Aid estimate for REA: mid-December; aid decision ~April 1 ✅.

### 4.3 Everything Harvard requires ✅
1. **Common App** + **5 Harvard short-answer questions, 150 words each**:
   1. How will the life experiences that shaped who you are enable you to contribute to Harvard?
   2. Describe a time you strongly disagreed with someone about an idea or issue — how did you communicate, and what did you learn?
   3. Briefly describe extracurriculars, employment, travel or family responsibilities that shaped who you are
   4. How do you hope to use your Harvard education in the future?
   5. Top 3 things your roommates might like to know about you
   (⚠️ prompts are for the current cycle; they may change.)
2. **Secondary School Report** (transcript + counselor letter + school profile if available)
3. **Midyear School Report**
   - If your curriculum is IB/A-level: school sends **predicted grades**
   - If your school doesn't issue midyear/predicted grades, you may not need it ✅
4. **Two teacher recommendations** from teachers in **different academic subjects** who know you well ✅
5. **SAT or ACT** (see 4.4)
6. **Final School Report + transcript** if admitted and enrolling — **by July 1** (IB: as soon as released mid-July; A-levels: mid-August) ✅ — cannot be emailed ✅
7. Optional supplemental materials (research, articles, music, art) — via Applicant Portal or SlideRoom ✅
8. Optional **alumni interview** — not available for all applicants; no penalty if you don't get one ✅
9. **Translated documents** (if originals aren't English): **don't translate them yourself**; an English teacher can translate; include translator's name and relationship; **always attach the original** ✅

### 4.4 Exams ✅
- **SAT or ACT required.** Only in "exceptional cases" (cannot access or afford a test site) can AP/IB/GCSE/A-level/national-leaving-exam results substitute. Harvard says it doesn't expect extraordinary measures — but if you *can* take the SAT, you're **expected to**.
- **English tests (TOEFL/IELTS/Duolingo) cannot replace the SAT/ACT** ✅
- **English proficiency exam: not required** for first-year applicants; optional to submit ✅
- Score reporting: **self-reporting allowed**; admitted enrollees send official scores. **Harvard codes: SAT 3434, ACT 1840** ✅
- **No cutoffs** ✅
- **Timing:** For REA, Harvard would like scores by end of October (November series OK); for RD, as late as February is possible but earlier is recommended ✅
- SAT/ACT fee waivers are generally **not available** to non-US students outside the US — Harvard says apply anyway if hardship ✅

### 4.5 Math/course guidance ✅
- No required courses. Recommends **4 years of math**; calculus is **not** required. For engineering/CS/physics, calculus helps.

### 4.6 Financial aid ✅ — one of the best for internationals
- **Need-blind for everyone including international students**: admission is decided without regard to aid request ✅
- Harvard **meets each student's demonstrated need** ✅
- International students are eligible for the **same amount of aid** as US students ✅
- **Travel allowance** is included in aid awards ✅
- **No merit scholarships** ✅
- **Grant-based, no required student loans** ⚠️ (from third-party; verify at college.harvard.edu/financial-aid/how-aid-works)
- **Forms** ✅:
  - **CSS Profile**, Harvard code **3434**. If parents are married/living together → one Profile. If divorced/separated → each parent files their own (or request a noncustodial parent waiver)
  - **IDOC** (you can't access IDOC until the CSS Profile is complete). Harvard's list is the definitive list, not IDOC's.
  - **Parents' income tax document for both parents** (the actual tax return **as filed with the government**, no drafts/estimates). For Indians: whatever your parents file with the Indian tax authority (ITR + any related statements). **If not in English, translate** — no official translator needed; students often write the translation directly on the document.
  - If parents don't file taxes: **Tax Non-Filer Statement** + wage statement / salary certificate / employer letter stating annual income
  - **Student's own income tax document** or non-filer statement
  - **Business/Farm Supplement** + business tax return for each business/farm (if parents own one)
  - **Trust/estate** documents if applicable
  - Optional letter explaining special circumstances
  - **Don't email documents** ✅
- **If you can't afford the CSS fee or College Board can't take payments in your country**, Harvard has an **alternate application** — see college.harvard.edu/alternate-application-financial-aid ✅
- **Harvard financial aid year matters:** tax-year requirement shifts yearly (the 2027-28 instructions ask for **2025** parent tax documents). For your cycle expect the **next** tax year — check the page when it updates.

### 4.7 Harvard-specific checklist
- [ ] SAT/ACT (before Oct/Nov for REA)
- [ ] Common App + essay + 5 Harvard short answers
- [ ] Fee or waiver
- [ ] School Report + counselor letter + transcript
- [ ] Midyear report (or predicted grades)
- [ ] 2 teacher recommendations (different subjects)
- [ ] CSS Profile (3434) by Nov 1 (REA) or Feb 1 (RD)
- [ ] IDOC: parent tax docs / non-filer statements / business forms / student tax doc
- [ ] Translations + originals
- [ ] Optional: research/portfolio uploads
- [ ] Final report by July 1 (if admitted)

## 5. CALIFORNIA INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY (CALTECH)

### 5.1 Platform & fee
- **Common App or Coalition App** ✅ (Caltech catalog)
- Fee ❓ (third-party figures conflict: $75 vs $85). **Fee waivers are offered if you are applying for aid and the fee is a hardship** ✅
- Caltech is **small**; the process is very STEM-preparation-focused.

### 5.2 Deadlines (2026-27 cycle) ✅

| Item | Date |
|---|---|
| Restrictive Early Action | **Nov 1, 2026** (decision mid-Dec) |
| Regular Decision | **Jan 4, 2027** (decision mid-March) |
| Reply date | **May 1, 2027** |

⚠️ **REA restriction:** if you apply REA to Caltech, you generally **may not apply Early Action or Early Decision to any other institution** (with limited exceptions) ✅ (Caltech catalog). Read the exceptions carefully before planning your early round.

### 5.3 Academic prerequisites ✅ (the strictest course expectations of the six)
Caltech expects mastery in:
- **4 years of math, including 1 year of calculus**
- **1 year physics**
- **1 year chemistry**
- 1 year biology (recommended)
- **4 years of English**
- 2 years of history/social science (3+ recommended)
- **If you can't take calculus/physics/chemistry** (not available/unresolvable conflict), Caltech will accept **exam scores or certification** *if both you and your counselor document the issue*.
- **For India specifically:** "Students in India must complete **both Year X and XII** state board or CBSE examinations." ✅
- IB: Higher Level Math; A-levels: A-level Maths ✅

### 5.4 Everything Caltech requires ✅
1. Common/Coalition application + **Caltech supplemental essays** (❓ prompts change yearly; Caltech also publishes an **AI-use policy** for essays — read it)
2. **Official transcript sent directly by your school** showing class titles, grades, predicted grades. If instruction isn't in English: **original + English translation**. "**We will not review your application if any part is not translated in English.**"
3. **Two teacher recommendations:** one **STEM** (math—calculus or beyond preferred, physics, chemistry, or biology), one **humanities/social science** (English, history, government, economics) ✅
4. **Mid-Year Report** (first-term senior grades) ✅
5. **SAT or ACT** — **required**; ACT writing/science not required ✅ (+ **AP/IB scores if taken**)
   - If you couldn't take SAT/ACT because it wasn't accessible, you get a chance to explain in the Caltech-specific application — but don't rely on this if you *can* test.
6. **English proficiency exam** (see 5.5)
7. **Optional supplemental materials** (research papers & publications, portfolio) ✅ (pages exist on Caltech's site)
8. **Optional InitialView** (video/writing sample) — *recommended for international applicants but not required*; fee waivers available; **does not replace** an English proficiency test ✅
9. International definition: non-US citizen/PR **attending secondary school outside the US** ✅

### 5.5 English proficiency ✅
Required for all internationals **unless** (1) your native language is English **or** (2) English is the **primary language of instruction** at your secondary school. If your school is English-medium, Caltech still **strongly recommends** a score. Accepted: DET, IELTS, TOEFL. **Must be taken before the application deadline.**

| Test | Minimum total | Minimum in each area |
|---|---|---|
| IELTS | 7 | 7 |
| TOEFL (before 21 Jan 2026) | 100 | 25 |
| TOEFL (on/after 21 Jan 2026) | 5 | 5 |
| Duolingo English Test | **130** | 130 |

(Note: Caltech's Duolingo minimum, 130, is higher than MIT's 120/125.)

### 5.6 Financial aid ⚠️ Need-aware — and not "all grants"
- **Need-aware for international students** ✅: "financial need may be a factor in admission decisions" because international aid funds are limited
- **You must apply for aid when you apply for admission** — if you don't (or you're denied aid for year 1), you **cannot apply for need-based aid at any other time** as an undergraduate ✅ (exception for Canada/Mexico)
- Caltech **meets 100% of demonstrated need** ✅ — **but through a combination of scholarships/grants, work-study and loans** ✅ ("Caltech does not award merit scholarships"). So for Caltech, "full aid" ≠ "zero loans/zero work" automatically.
- Fall 2026 class: **average grant aid just under $69,000**; most students from families earning **under $100,000** (typical assets) get a **no-loan** package covering tuition, fees, housing and food; **under $200,000** → tuition typically covered ✅ (statements are mostly about the overall/domestic class; confirm what applies to internationals with the aid office)
- **International aid forms (for the Fall 2026 entry cycle, RD) ✅** — new for each cycle:
  - **CSS Profile** (Caltech code **4034**) — deadline **Feb 15, 2026** (RD)
  - **Parents' Non-Tax Filer's Statement for Foreign Filer or Foreign Non-Filer** — by **Mar 16, 2026**
  - **After matriculation (by July 15):** Parents' national tax return + **Scholarship Data Form & Addendum**
  - The REA aid timeline is on a separate Caltech page ❓ — check finaid.caltech.edu/applying/how/newinternationalrea
- There is reportedly **no ISFAA/paper alternative** to the CSS Profile for Caltech ⚠️ (third-party); Caltech assesses your CSS fee-waiver request and may email a coupon code ⚠️

### 5.7 Caltech-specific checklist
- [ ] Verify you will have finished **calculus, physics, chemistry, 4 yrs English, 2 yrs history/social science** — and your **Class X and XII board** results
- [ ] SAT/ACT
- [ ] English proficiency test (IELTS 7/7 each, TOEFL 5 new scale each section, or DET 130) — taken **before** deadline
- [ ] Optional: InitialView
- [ ] Application + supplemental essays
- [ ] Transcript from school (original + translation if needed)
- [ ] 2 teacher recs: 1 STEM + 1 humanities
- [ ] Mid-year report
- [ ] Tick "apply for financial aid" (irreversible)
- [ ] CSS Profile (4034) by the published aid deadline + non-tax-filer statement; July 15 forms after matriculation
- [ ] Optional: research paper/portfolio uploads

## 6. YALE UNIVERSITY

### 6.1 Platform & fee
- **Common App**, **Coalition App on SCOIR**, or **QuestBridge** — submit only one per cycle ✅
- Fee ⚠️ (third-party says $80). Yale doesn't issue its own waivers; you get waivers through the platform's eligibility rules (Common App/Coalition). The QuestBridge application is always free ✅

### 6.2 Deadlines ✅

| Round | Deadline |
|---|---|

## 8. FINANCIAL AID & "100% SCHOLARSHIP" — THE HONEST PICTURE

### 8.1 How "100%" works
- Total cost (tuition + housing + food + fees + books + personal + travel) at these schools is roughly **$90,000–$105,000 per year** ⚠️ (third-party cost figures: MIT $92,760 for 2026-27; Princeton ~$103,820 in one aggregator; verify each).
- Aid = **Cost of attendance – family's expected contribution**. If the family contribution is low/zero → aid ≈ full cost. If it's higher → you pay the difference. The "scholarship" has no merit component; no award can exceed need.

### 8.2 Comparison

| | Need-blind? | Aid type | Key form | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MIT | ⚠️ Yes (verify) | Need-based MIT Scholarship ✅ | CSS (3514) + IDOC | Very low international admit rate (~2%) |
| Stanford | **No (need-aware)** ✅ | Need-based; meets full need if admitted | CSS (4704) + Int'l Supplement | Requesting aid lowers your odds; **can't request later** |
| Harvard | **Yes** ✅ | Need-based; grant-heavy ⚠️ | CSS (3434) + IDOC | Document completeness |
| Caltech | **No (need-aware)** ✅ | Grants + work-study + loans ✅ | CSS (4034) + 2 more forms | Requesting aid lowers your odds; loans/work possible |
| Yale | **Yes** ✅ | Need-based; allowances for housing/meals/travel ✅ | CSS (3987) + IDOC; Yale sends checklist | Early aid deadline for internationals (Dec 1) |
| Princeton | **Yes** ✅ | Grants, no loans ✅ | PFAA (**not** CSS) | Must file PFAA before Nov 9/Feb 1 |

### 8.3 What this means for strategy
- **Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT**: you can request full aid with no admission penalty.
- **Stanford and Caltech**: asking for aid hurts your admission chances compared with a "full-pay" international applicant, and the choice is **permanent for all four years**. If you truly need aid, you must request it — just know this is the cost.
- **Do not hide or understate financial need.** Admission offices cross-check; false statements can get an offer rescinded (Harvard states this explicitly for any application misrepresentation).

### 8.4 Other funding (not verified)
- All six allow outside scholarships (Caltech explicitly says you're "welcome and encouraged" to apply for them ✅). I did not research Indian external scholarship programs — ❓ **research this separately** (e.g., country-specific foundations, Indian government/education-loan options, and any private scholarships open to undergraduates going abroad).
- After admission: student visa (F-1) requires proof of funds and an I-20 issued by the university. (General knowledge, not from the pages above.) Stanford says visa details are sent after an offer ✅.
